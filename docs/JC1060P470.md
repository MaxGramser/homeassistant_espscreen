# Guition JC1060P470, 7 inch, experimental

Added in ESP Screen Manager 0.3.6, firmware 0.3.1.
Firmware 0.3.4 (app 0.3.9) gives the first panel a hardware reset, so it starts again after a power cut.
This is the **Guition JC1060P470**, also sold as **JC1060P470C_I_W_Y**: a 7-inch 1024 x 600 IPS panel with a JD9165
MIPI-DSI driver, GT911 capacitive touch, an ESP32-P4 with 16 MB flash and PSRAM, and an ESP32-C6 beside it for
Wi-Fi. Physical acceptance has not been performed: the firmware is built and rendered, but nobody on the project has
run it on the glass yet.

## Which of the two

Guition ships this board with two different LCDs behind the same driver chip. Look at the label on the back of the case:

- **No "V2" after the material number**: choose **Guition · 7 inch, JC1060P470**.
- **"V2" after the material number** (boards made since about mid-2026): choose **Guition · 7 inch, JC1060P470 V2**.

When the label does not settle it, the four-digit date code on the screen's own circuit board, behind the back cover,
does: community reports put 2622 and later on the V2.

The two firmwares differ only in how they start the panel and in the clock of the Wi-Fi link. The wrong one does no
harm: a V2 panel started as the first one shows a white screen with a vertical band of noise. Flash the other one
over USB and the screen comes up.

## Install

Update ESP Screen Manager and choose the 7-inch Guition that matches the label (marked Experimental) in **New screen**.
Follow [Easy setup](EASY_SETUP.md) to create a profile with its own name, Wi-Fi references and unique API/OTA keys.
Choose the correct USB port, or download the firmware to flash with ESPHome Web from your own computer.
Keep an existing working profile if the board is already installed. Pair it through the ESPHome integration before
adding tiles.

The remote packages are `packages/jc1060p470.yaml` and `packages/jc1060p470v2.yaml`; the checkout entries are
`checkout/jc1060p470.yaml` and `checkout/jc1060p470v2.yaml`. Each combines `packages/core.yaml` with its board file,
`packages/boards/guition-jc1060p470.yaml` or `packages/boards/guition-jc1060p470-v2.yaml`. What the two share lives in
`packages/hardware/guition-jc1060p470.yaml`, and what they share with the 10.1-inch Guition (the ESP32-P4 and its
ESP32-C6) in `packages/hardware/guition-esp32p4.yaml`.

## Layout and capabilities

- The glass has the size and resolution of a 7-inch 1024 x 600 panel at 170 dpi, the density of the 4-inch Guition, so
  a tile keeps the same size in millimetres: four columns of four lying down, two columns of seven standing up, as on
  the 7-inch Waveshare.
- The panel is 1024 x 600 itself, so it lies down without a turn. Standing up is a quarter turn, chosen when the screen
  is built.
- The backlight is a PWM pin (GPIO23): brightness is a percentage, and standby, night mode, Sleep, Wake and the alert's
  flashes are enabled.
- Camera tiles, full-screen snapshots, live tile pictures, camera alerts and media artwork use the shared PSRAM
  implementation.
- The GT911 reports pixel coordinates, with no calibration. The existing touch filter and action guard stay in use.
- The audio chips (ES8311, ES7210), the speaker amplifier, the microphone, the SD card slot and the RGB status LED on the
  board stay unused.
- The pixels of this panel are about 5 % wider than tall, so a circle looks a little wide. The firmware does not correct
  for that.

## Hardware references

- [ESPHome MIPI DSI](https://esphome.io/components/display/mipi_dsi/): the first panel uses the init sequence and
  timings of ESPHome's own `model: JC1060P470`, written out in full so the panel gets a hardware reset and a 120 ms wait
  before its init table. With the model alone it gets a software reset and the table straight after it, and after a cold
  start the firmware could hang there until the watchdog restarted it, on every boot.
- The V2 panel has no ESPHome model yet. Its timings and init table are those of Guition's own driver for the new panel,
  as [ESPHome pull request 18695](https://github.com/esphome/esphome/pull/18695) carries them, with the 120 ms wait after
  the reset that the panel needs to come back after a restart.
- The pins follow the configurations the ESPHome community runs on this board. They agree on:
  - Wi-Fi: ESP32-C6 over four-bit SDIO, reset GPIO54, CMD GPIO19, CLK GPIO18, D0 to D3 GPIO14 to GPIO17. The link runs at
    20 MHz on the first panel and at 10 MHz on the V2, where 20 MHz puts vertical streaks into the picture.
  - The MIPI-DSI PHY takes its supply from the chip's internal regulator on channel 3, at 2.5 V.
  - I2C: SDA GPIO7, SCL GPIO8, 400 kHz. GT911 at 0x5D with reset GPIO22 and interrupt GPIO21.
  - Backlight: GPIO23, PWM.
- The panel's reset line is GPIO0 on the schematic. Both panels use it.
- ESP32-P4 silicon before revision 3 and revision 3 itself are not binary compatible. The firmware is built for the
  earlier silicon (`engineering_sample: true`, 360 MHz), which is what the boards shipped so far report in their boot log
  ("chip revision: v1.3"). A board that reports v3.0 or higher needs `engineering_sample: false` in its Override YAML.

## What to report while testing

1. Which of the two you have (the label), the chip revision from the first lines of the USB log, a successful boot,
   pairing and appearance in ESP Screens.
2. Correct colours and a stable picture across several page changes, a restart from the screen's settings, an update
   over the air and a cold start. A white or noisy screen after a restart that kept the power on is the thing to look for.
3. Physical taps near each corner, slider drags and edge swipes. Touch that lands mirrored or swapped points at the touch
   transform (`TOUCH_SWAP_XY`, `TOUCH_MIRROR_X`, `TOUCH_MIRROR_Y`).
4. Whether the picture is the right way up for how the case is meant to stand. If it is upside down, the screen's
   Rotation setting (180°) turns picture and touch together; say which way round was right.
5. The brightness setting across its range, standby after the timeout and the wake by touch, several times in a row,
   and night mode. Report any restart, a dark screen that does not wake, or lost touch after a wake.
6. A full page of tiles, opening and closing the settings and detail cards.
7. Camera tile pictures, full-screen camera images and camera alerts, including repeated opens and closes.
8. `sensor.<screen>_heap_free` with a full layout, any reset, Wi-Fi drop or I2C error, and how long the screen stayed
   running.

Do not publish Wi-Fi passwords or API/OTA keys with logs.
