# Guition JC1060P470, 7 inch, experimental

Added in app 0.3.6, firmware 0.3.1.
Firmware 0.3.4 (app 0.3.9) gives the first panel a hardware reset, so it starts again after a power cut.
This is the **Guition JC1060P470**, also sold as **JC1060P470C_I_W_Y**: a 7-inch 1024 x 600 IPS panel with a JD9165
MIPI-DSI driver, GT911 capacitive touch, an ESP32-P4 with 16 MB flash and PSRAM, and an ESP32-C6 beside it for
Wi-Fi. Physical acceptance has not been performed: the firmware is built and rendered, but nobody on the project has
run it on the glass yet.

## Which of the two

Guition ships this board with two different LCDs behind the same driver chip. Look at the label on the back of the case:

- **No "V2" after the material number**: choose **Guition · 7 inch, JC1060P470**.
- **"V2" after the material number** (boards made since about mid-2026): choose **Guition · 7 inch, JC1060P470 V2**.

When the label does not settle it, the four-digit date code on the screen's own circuit board, behind the back cover,
does: community reports put 2622 and later on the V2.

The two firmwares differ only in how they start the panel and in the clock of the Wi-Fi link. The wrong one does no
harm: a V2 panel started as the first one shows a white screen with a vertical band of noise. Flash the other one
over USB and the screen comes up.

### Black case or yellow board

The first panel (not the V2) also comes in two builds, and **New screen** asks which one under **Board**:

- **Black, in a case** (JC1060P470C_I_W_Y): the board built on Guition's JC-ESP32P4-M3 module, inside a black case.
  This is the default.
- **Yellow, without a case** (JC1060P470C_I_W): a yellow circuit board with the bare ESP32-P4 and ESP32-C6 chips, sold
  as a 7-inch "CYD". Its GT911 reports the corners half a turn round: with the black board's settings a tap on the top
  left lands on the bottom right, on every rotation. The yellow choice turns the touch round and gives the audio the
  pins of this board, which are those of the 10.1-inch Guition.

A screen set up before this choice keeps the black board's settings. On a yellow board, these lines in its Override
YAML do the same as the choice:

```yaml
substitutions:
  BOARD_VARIANT: "yellow"
```

## Install

Update Tessera Screen Manager and choose the 7-inch Guition that matches the label (marked Experimental) in **New screen**.
Follow [Easy setup](EASY_SETUP.md) to create a profile with its own name, Wi-Fi references and unique API/OTA keys.
Choose the correct USB port, or download the firmware to flash with ESPHome Web from your own computer.
Keep an existing working profile if the board is already installed. Tessera adds it to Home Assistant by itself once it is on
your Wi-Fi; then add its tiles.

The remote packages are `packages/jc1060p470.yaml` and `packages/jc1060p470v2.yaml`; the checkout entries are
`checkout/jc1060p470.yaml` and `checkout/jc1060p470v2.yaml`. Each combines `packages/core.yaml` with its board file,
`packages/boards/guition-jc1060p470.yaml` or `packages/boards/guition-jc1060p470-v2.yaml`. What the two share lives in
`packages/hardware/guition-jc1060p470.yaml`, and what they share with the 10.1-inch Guition (the ESP32-P4 and its
ESP32-C6) in `packages/hardware/guition-esp32p4.yaml`.

## Layout and capabilities

- The glass has the size and resolution of a 7-inch 1024 x 600 panel at 170 dpi, the density of the 4-inch Guition, so
  a tile keeps the same size in millimetres: four columns of four lying down, two columns of seven standing up, as on
  the 7-inch Waveshare.
- The panel is 1024 x 600 itself, so it lies down without a turn. Standing up is a quarter turn, chosen when the screen
  is set up and changed later in the editor (firmware 0.53.0+).
- The backlight is a PWM pin (GPIO23): brightness is a percentage, and standby, night mode, Sleep, Wake and the alert's
  flashes are enabled.
- Camera tiles, full-screen snapshots, live tile pictures, camera alerts and media artwork use the shared PSRAM
  implementation.
- The GT911 reports pixel coordinates, with no calibration. The existing touch filter and action guard stay in use.
- The speaker and the microphone are the screen's audio, experimental: a media player in Home Assistant for
  announcements and music, Volume and Microphone under Screen settings, and what audio plugins (a voice assistant)
  need. See "Speaker and microphone" below.
- The SD card slot and the RGB status LED on the board stay unused.
- The pixels of this panel are about 5 % wider than tall, so a circle looks a little wide. The firmware does not correct
  for that.

## Speaker and microphone

The board has one ES8311 codec, an NS4150 amplifier with a two-pin speaker connector, and one analogue MEMS
microphone. There is no ES7210 and no second microphone. Both 7-inch board files take the audio every Guition ESP32-P4
panel shares (`packages/hardware/guition-p4-audio.yaml`), with the pins of this board:

- The speaker and the microphone work at the same time, at 16 kHz. The codec has one I2S bus, and two of the chip's
  I2S ports share its clock pins: the microphone's port gives the clocks and the speaker's follows them (ESPHome's
  `i2s_mode: secondary`). The microphone's port runs from the start, so the speaker always has its clock, also when
  nothing listens. There is no echo cancellation: the board has nothing that hears what the speaker plays, so a voice
  assistant hears the speaker too.
- The bus runs all the time, in silence between sounds, so the amplifier stays on. Switching it with every sound cut
  off the start of a short one, such as the click of a tap.
- The volume stops at the codec's 0 dB. Above it a loud sound clips, and on a 10.1-inch Guition full volume was found
  to distort and to brown out the board.
- The microphone is analogue, on the ES8311's own input, so the codec keeps its analogue input (`use_microphone: false`
  in ESPHome's `es8311`, which would switch it to a PDM microphone) and the microphone is read on the left I2S slot
  with the codec's digital gain at 24 dB, Guition's own setting.

This is **experimental**. [@Eugen417](https://github.com/Eugen417) runs the same codec wiring, both ways at once,
on a 10.1-inch Guition JC8012P4A1 V3 (#259): the tap sound and a voice assistant plugin work. On this board's pins
nobody has tried it yet.

The board in the black case is built on the JC-ESP32P4-M3 module, whose panel reset is GPIO0. The yellow board without
a case (JC1060P470C_I_W, "Black case or yellow board" above) has the audio pins of the 10.1-inch Guition, which its
choice in New screen gives it: the microphone's data on GPIO11 and the amplifier's enable on GPIO20. Its panel reset is
GPIO27 on Guition's schematic. The firmware still resets GPIO0, and the yellow board has started fine that way so far,
so the panel reset stays as it is until someone with that board tries GPIO27. Nobody has tried the audio on a yellow
board yet; please report how it goes.

## The camera

The camera version of this board (the "C" in Guition's model name, JC1060P470C) carries an OmniVision OV02C10 on the
MIPI-CSI port. It answers on the touch bus at 0x36, clocks itself and has no reset or power-down pin; the board file
names that bus in `CAMERA_I2C`, the feature `camera_sensor` (docs/PLUGINS.md). Espressif's own `esp_cam_sensor` has no
driver for the OV02C10, so the Screen camera plugin brings one (its `components/ov02c10`, with an image tuning for this
sensor) beside the camera driver, and makes it a camera of the screen's device in Home Assistant: a 1288 x 728 picture,
a stream at up to five pictures a second.

- **A board without the camera** fits Screen camera as well: `CAMERA_I2C` says where a camera answers, not that one is
  there. Without a sensor the camera only logs that it found none.
- **Untried in Tessera.** Only the V2 names its camera so far; Guition's firmware for the JC1060P470C drives the
  OV02C10 on these pins (espressif/esp-video-components#90). The first JC1060P470 is left out until someone finds
  where the yellow board's camera answers.
- **A protective film** may come on the lens of a new board and make the picture hazy.

## Hardware references

- [ESPHome MIPI DSI](https://esphome.io/components/display/mipi_dsi/): the first panel uses the init sequence and
  timings of ESPHome's own `model: JC1060P470`, written out in full so the panel gets a hardware reset and a 120 ms wait
  before its init table. With the model alone it gets a software reset and the table straight after it, and after a cold
  start the firmware could hang there until the watchdog restarted it, on every boot.
- The V2 panel has no ESPHome model yet. Its timings and init table are those of Guition's own driver for the new panel,
  as [ESPHome pull request 18695](https://github.com/esphome/esphome/pull/18695) carries them, with the 120 ms wait after
  the reset that the panel needs to come back after a restart.
- The pins follow the configurations the ESPHome community runs on this board. They agree on:
  - Wi-Fi: ESP32-C6 over four-bit SDIO, reset GPIO54, CMD GPIO19, CLK GPIO18, D0 to D3 GPIO14 to GPIO17. The link runs at
    20 MHz on the first panel and at 10 MHz on the V2, where 20 MHz puts vertical streaks into the picture.
  - The MIPI-DSI PHY takes its supply from the chip's internal regulator on channel 3, at 2.5 V.
  - I2C: SDA GPIO7, SCL GPIO8, 400 kHz. GT911 at 0x5D with reset GPIO22 and interrupt GPIO21.
  - Backlight: GPIO23, PWM.
  - Audio: ES8311 at 0x18 on the same I2C bus. I2S MCLK GPIO13, BCLK GPIO12, LRCLK GPIO10, speaker data GPIO9 (the
    codec's DSDIN), microphone data GPIO48 (its ASDOUT), at 16 kHz. Amplifier enable GPIO11, high for on, with a
    pull-down that keeps it off at reset.
- The panel's reset line is GPIO0 on the schematic. Both panels use it.
- The audio follows Guition's own documents in its
  [P4-series repository](https://github.com/guitionofficial/P4-series/tree/9f1510954d281e30b19cd8baf115e2ed2a65a90a):
  the JC1060P470C_I_W_Y schematic (`5-Schematic/JC1060P470C_I_W_Y-V1.0.pdf`) and the pins in its board support
  package (`esp32_p4_function_ev_board.h`). The community firmware
  [HomeTiles](https://github.com/GalusPeres/HomeTiles) drives the speaker of these boards the same way
  (`src/devices/*/speaker_board.h`).
- This board needs ESPHome 2026.9.0 or newer for its audio.
- ESP32-P4 silicon before revision 3 and revision 3 itself are not binary compatible. The firmware is built for the
  earlier silicon (`engineering_sample: true`, 360 MHz), which is what the boards shipped so far report in their boot log
  ("chip revision: v1.3"). A board that reports v3.0 or higher needs `engineering_sample: false` in its Override YAML.

## What to report while testing

1. Which of the two you have (the label), the chip revision from the first lines of the USB log, a successful boot
   and appearance in Tessera.
2. Correct colours and a stable picture across several page changes, a restart from the screen's settings, an update
   over the air and a cold start. A white or noisy screen after a restart that kept the power on is the thing to look for.
3. Physical taps near each corner, slider drags and edge swipes. Touch that lands mirrored or swapped points at the touch
   transform (`TOUCH_SWAP_XY`, `TOUCH_MIRROR_X`, `TOUCH_MIRROR_Y`).
4. Whether the picture is the right way up for how the case is meant to stand. If it is upside down, the screen's
   Rotation setting (180°) turns picture and touch together; say which way round was right.
5. The brightness setting across its range, standby after the timeout and the wake by touch, several times in a row,
   and night mode. Report any restart, a dark screen that does not wake, or lost touch after a wake.
6. A full page of tiles, opening and closing the settings and detail cards.
7. Camera tile pictures, full-screen camera images and camera alerts, including repeated opens and closes.
8. A text-to-speech announcement and music on the screen's media player, without a voice assistant plugin as well, the
   Volume setting from low to high, and whether the speaker hisses between sounds.
9. The microphone, with a voice assistant plugin: whether the wake word is heard from across the room, also while
   music plays.
10. `sensor.<screen>_heap_free` with a full layout, any reset, Wi-Fi drop or I2C error, and how long the screen stayed
    running.

Do not publish Wi-Fi passwords or API/OTA keys with logs.
