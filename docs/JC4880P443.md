# Guition JC4880P443, 4.3 inch, experimental

This is the **Guition JC4880P443**: a 4.3-inch 480 x 800 IPS panel with an ST7701 MIPI-DSI driver, GT911 capacitive
touch, an ESP32-P4 with 16 MB flash and PSRAM, and an ESP32-C6 beside it for Wi-Fi.

It has run on a real board, a pre-v3 one: touch, colours, rotation and the backlight work. Standby, night mode, camera
pictures and a long run with a full layout have not been checked yet (see "What to report while testing" below).

## Which board

Only the first build of this board is supported, with an ESP32-P4 before silicon revision 3. Guition also sells a
**10150002-V3**, with rev3 silicon (boot logs report v3.x). The firmware does not boot on that one, and a successful
flash over USB does not mean otherwise. The chip revision is in the first lines of the USB log ("chip revision: v1.3").

## Install

Update Tessera Screen Manager and choose **Guition · 4.3 inch, JC4880P443** (marked Experimental) in **New screen**.
Follow [Easy setup](EASY_SETUP.md) to create a profile with its own name, Wi-Fi references and unique API/OTA keys.
Choose the correct USB port, or download the firmware to flash with ESPHome Web from your own computer.
Keep an existing working profile if the board is already installed. Tessera adds it to Home Assistant by itself once it is on
your Wi-Fi; then add its tiles.

The remote package is `packages/jc4880p443.yaml` and the checkout entry `checkout/jc4880p443.yaml`. Each combines
`packages/core.yaml` with the board file `packages/boards/guition-jc4880p443.yaml`. What it shares with the 10.1-inch
and the 7-inch Guition (the ESP32-P4, its ESP32-C6, the touch bus and the backlight's pin) lives in
`packages/hardware/guition-esp32p4.yaml`.

## Layout and capabilities

- The glass has the size and resolution of the Waveshare 4.3-inch, 800 x 480 at 217 dpi, so it takes the same grid:
  three columns of three lying down, one column of four standing up.
- The panel is 480 x 800 itself, a portrait panel. Lying down is a quarter turn, standing up a half turn, chosen when
  the screen is built. The standing build is the way up the board is usually sold and stood.
- The backlight is a PWM pin (GPIO23): brightness is a percentage, and standby, night mode, Sleep, Wake and the alert's
  flashes are enabled.
- Camera tiles, full-screen snapshots, live tile pictures, camera alerts and media artwork use the shared PSRAM
  implementation.
- The GT911 reports pixel coordinates, with no calibration. The existing touch filter and action guard stay in use.
- The speaker and the microphone are the screen's audio, experimental: a media player in Home Assistant for
  announcements and music, Volume and Microphone under Screen settings, and what audio plugins (a voice assistant)
  need. See "Speaker and microphone" below.

## Speaker and microphone

The board has one ES8311 codec, an NS4150 amplifier with a two-pin speaker connector, and one analogue MEMS
microphone. There is no ES7210 and no second microphone. The board file takes the audio every Guition ESP32-P4 panel
shares (`packages/hardware/guition-p4-audio.yaml`), with the pins of the JC-ESP32P4-M3 module this board is built on,
the same as the 7-inch Guition's:

- The speaker and the microphone work at the same time, at 16 kHz. The codec has one I2S bus, and two of the chip's
  I2S ports share its clock pins: the microphone's port gives the clocks and the speaker's follows them (ESPHome's
  `i2s_mode: secondary`). The microphone's port runs from the start, so the speaker always has its clock, also when
  nothing listens. There is no echo cancellation: the board has nothing that hears what the speaker plays, so a voice
  assistant hears the speaker too.
- The bus runs all the time, in silence between sounds, so the amplifier stays on. Switching it with every sound cut
  off the start of a short one, such as the click of a tap.
- The volume stops at the codec's 0 dB. Above it a loud sound clips, and on a 10.1-inch Guition full volume was found
  to distort and to brown out the board.
- The microphone is analogue, on the ES8311's own input, so the codec keeps its analogue input (`use_microphone: false`
  in ESPHome's `es8311`, which would switch it to a PDM microphone) and the microphone is read on the left I2S slot
  with the codec's digital gain at 24 dB, Guition's own setting.

This is **experimental**. [@Eugen417](https://github.com/Eugen417) runs the same codec wiring, both ways at once,
on a 10.1-inch Guition JC8012P4A1 V3 (#259): the tap sound and a voice assistant plugin work. On this board's pins
nobody has tried it yet.

## The camera

The camera version of this board (the "C" in Guition's model name, JC4880P443C) carries an OmniVision OV02C10 on the
MIPI-CSI port. It answers on the touch bus at 0x36, clocks itself and has no reset or power-down pin; the board file
names that bus in `CAMERA_I2C`, the feature `camera_sensor` (docs/PLUGINS.md). Espressif's own `esp_cam_sensor` has no
driver for the OV02C10, so the Screen camera plugin brings one (its `components/ov02c10`, with an image tuning for this
sensor) beside the camera driver, and makes it a camera of the screen's device in Home Assistant: a 1288 x 728 picture,
a stream at up to five pictures a second.

- **A board without the camera** fits Screen camera as well: `CAMERA_I2C` says where a camera answers, not that one is
  there. Without a sensor the camera only logs that it found none.
- **Untried in Tessera.** Guition's own camera example for the JC4880P443C uses these pins and this sensor. The
  boards Tessera knows of have pre-v3 silicon, whose image processor corrects no black level: Screen camera picks
  the tuning for that chip by itself.
- **A protective film** may come on the lens of a new board and make the picture hazy.

## Hardware references

- [ESPHome MIPI DSI](https://esphome.io/components/display/mipi_dsi/): the panel uses ESPHome's own
  `model: JC4880P443`, which brings the ST7701's init sequence, its timings and its reset pin (GPIO5).
- The pins follow the configurations the ESPHome community runs on this board:
  - Wi-Fi: ESP32-C6 over four-bit SDIO, reset GPIO54, CMD GPIO19, CLK GPIO18, D0 to D3 GPIO14 to GPIO17, at 20 MHz.
  - The MIPI-DSI PHY takes its supply from the chip's internal regulator on channel 3, at 2.5 V.
  - I2C: SDA GPIO7, SCL GPIO8, 400 kHz, for the GT911. No reset or interrupt pin is wired for it in those configurations.
  - Backlight: GPIO23, PWM.
- Audio: ES8311 at 0x18 on the touch panel's I2C bus. I2S MCLK GPIO13, BCLK GPIO12, LRCLK GPIO10, speaker data GPIO9
  (the codec's DSDIN), microphone data GPIO48 (its ASDOUT), at 16 kHz. Amplifier enable GPIO11, high for on, with a
  pull-down that keeps it off at reset. This follows Guition's own documents in its
  [P4-series repository](https://github.com/guitionofficial/P4-series/tree/9f1510954d281e30b19cd8baf115e2ed2a65a90a):
  the JC4880P443C_I_W schematic (`5-Schematic/6_CODEC&TFCARD.png`) and the pins in its board support package
  (`esp32_p4_function_ev_board.h`). The community firmware [HomeTiles](https://github.com/GalusPeres/HomeTiles) drives
  the speaker of these boards the same way (`src/devices/*/speaker_board.h`).
- ESP32-P4 silicon before revision 3 and revision 3 itself are not binary compatible. The firmware is built for the
  earlier silicon (`engineering_sample: true`, 360 MHz).
- ESPHome 2026.9.0 or newer, for the audio.

## What to report while testing

1. The chip revision from the first lines of the USB log, a successful boot and appearance in Tessera.
2. Correct colours and a stable picture across several page changes, a restart from the screen's settings, an update
   over the air and a cold start.
3. Physical taps near each corner, slider drags and edge swipes. Touch that lands mirrored or swapped points at the touch
   transform (`TOUCH_SWAP_XY`, `TOUCH_MIRROR_X`, `TOUCH_MIRROR_Y`).
4. Whether the picture is the right way up, lying down and standing up. If it is upside down, the screen's Rotation
   setting (180°) turns picture and touch together; say which way round was right.
5. The brightness setting across its range, standby after the timeout and the wake by touch, several times in a row,
   and night mode.
6. A full page of tiles, opening and closing the settings and detail cards.
7. Camera tile pictures, full-screen camera images and camera alerts, including repeated opens and closes.
8. A text-to-speech announcement and music on the screen's media player, without a voice assistant plugin as well, the
   Volume setting from low to high, and whether the speaker hisses between sounds.
9. The microphone, with a voice assistant plugin: whether the wake word is heard from across the room, also while
   music plays.
10. `sensor.<screen>_heap_free` with a full layout, any reset, Wi-Fi drop or I2C error, and how long the screen stayed
    running.

Do not publish Wi-Fi passwords or API/OTA keys with logs.
