# Guition JC4880P443, 4.3 inch, experimental

This is the **Guition JC4880P443**: a 4.3-inch 480 x 800 IPS panel with an ST7701 MIPI-DSI driver, GT911 capacitive
touch, an ESP32-P4 with 16 MB flash and PSRAM, and an ESP32-C6 beside it for Wi-Fi. Physical acceptance has not been
performed: the firmware is built and rendered, but nobody on the project has run it on the glass yet.

## Which board

Only the first build of this board is supported, with an ESP32-P4 before silicon revision 3. Guition also sells a
**10150002-V3**, with rev3 silicon (boot logs report v3.x). The firmware does not boot on that one, and a successful
flash over USB does not mean otherwise. The chip revision is in the first lines of the USB log ("chip revision: v1.3").

## Install

Update Tessera Screen Manager and choose **Guition · 4.3 inch, JC4880P443** (marked Experimental) in **New screen**.
Follow [Easy setup](EASY_SETUP.md) to create a profile with its own name, Wi-Fi references and unique API/OTA keys.
Choose the correct USB port, or download the firmware to flash with ESPHome Web from your own computer.
Keep an existing working profile if the board is already installed. Tessera adds it to Home Assistant by itself once it is on
your Wi-Fi; then add its tiles.

The remote package is `packages/jc4880p443.yaml` and the checkout entry `checkout/jc4880p443.yaml`. Each combines
`packages/core.yaml` with the board file `packages/boards/guition-jc4880p443.yaml`. What it shares with the 10.1-inch
and the 7-inch Guition (the ESP32-P4, its ESP32-C6, the touch bus and the backlight's pin) lives in
`packages/hardware/guition-esp32p4.yaml`.

## Layout and capabilities

- The glass has the size and resolution of the Waveshare 4.3-inch, 800 x 480 at 217 dpi, so it takes the same grid:
  three columns of three lying down, one column of four standing up.
- The panel is 480 x 800 itself, a portrait panel. Lying down is a quarter turn, standing up a half turn, chosen when
  the screen is built. The standing build is the way up the board is usually sold and stood.
- The backlight is a PWM pin (GPIO23): brightness is a percentage, and standby, night mode, Sleep, Wake and the alert's
  flashes are enabled.
- Camera tiles, full-screen snapshots, live tile pictures, camera alerts and media artwork use the shared PSRAM
  implementation.
- The GT911 reports pixel coordinates, with no calibration. The existing touch filter and action guard stay in use.

## Hardware references

- [ESPHome MIPI DSI](https://esphome.io/components/display/mipi_dsi/): the panel uses ESPHome's own
  `model: JC4880P443`, which brings the ST7701's init sequence, its timings and its reset pin (GPIO5).
- The pins follow the configurations the ESPHome community runs on this board:
  - Wi-Fi: ESP32-C6 over four-bit SDIO, reset GPIO54, CMD GPIO19, CLK GPIO18, D0 to D3 GPIO14 to GPIO17, at 20 MHz.
  - The MIPI-DSI PHY takes its supply from the chip's internal regulator on channel 3, at 2.5 V.
  - I2C: SDA GPIO7, SCL GPIO8, 400 kHz, for the GT911. No reset or interrupt pin is wired for it in those configurations.
  - Backlight: GPIO23, PWM.
- ESP32-P4 silicon before revision 3 and revision 3 itself are not binary compatible. The firmware is built for the
  earlier silicon (`engineering_sample: true`, 360 MHz).

## What to report while testing

1. The chip revision from the first lines of the USB log, a successful boot and appearance in Tessera.
2. Correct colours and a stable picture across several page changes, a restart from the screen's settings, an update
   over the air and a cold start.
3. Physical taps near each corner, slider drags and edge swipes. Touch that lands mirrored or swapped points at the touch
   transform (`TOUCH_SWAP_XY`, `TOUCH_MIRROR_X`, `TOUCH_MIRROR_Y`).
4. Whether the picture is the right way up, lying down and standing up. If it is upside down, the screen's Rotation
   setting (180°) turns picture and touch together; say which way round was right.
5. The brightness setting across its range, standby after the timeout and the wake by touch, several times in a row,
   and night mode.
6. A full page of tiles, opening and closing the settings and detail cards.
7. Camera tile pictures, full-screen camera images and camera alerts, including repeated opens and closes.
8. `sensor.<screen>_heap_free` with a full layout, any reset, Wi-Fi drop or I2C error, and how long the screen stayed
   running.

Do not publish Wi-Fi passwords or API/OTA keys with logs.
