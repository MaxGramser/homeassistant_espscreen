# Wireless-Tag WT32-SC01, 3.5 inch, experimental

The **WT32-SC01** from Wireless-Tag is a 3.5-inch 480 x 320 board: an ST7796S panel over SPI, FT6336U capacitive touch
over I2C, and a classic ESP32 (an ESP32-WROVER-B module: 16 MB flash and PSRAM). It is not the newer
[WT32-SC01 Plus](WT32SC01PLUS.md), which has an ESP32-S3, an 8-bit panel bus and other pins.

A community member has run this board file on the board (see "Who tried what" below). The project has not tried it on
one, so the board is **experimental**.

## Install

Update Tessera Screen Manager and choose **WT32 · 3.5 inch, WT32-SC01** (marked Experimental) in **New screen**. Follow
[Easy setup](EASY_SETUP.md) to create a profile with its own name, Wi-Fi references and unique API/OTA keys, then flash
it over USB or download the firmware for ESPHome Web. There is no touch calibration: the touch panel reports pixels.

The remote package is `packages/wt32sc01.yaml` and the checkout entry `checkout/wt32sc01.yaml`. Both combine
`packages/core.yaml` with the board file `packages/boards/wt32sc01.yaml`.

## Layout and capabilities

- Lying down 480 x 320, two columns of two cells; standing up 320 x 480, one column of three. The standard look at
  165 dpi, the size and resolution of the WT32-SC01 Plus and the Waveshare 3.5-inch.
- The backlight is a PWM pin (GPIO23) at 1 kHz: brightness is a percentage, and standby, night mode, Sleep, Wake and the
  alert's flashes are enabled.
- The FT6336U reports pixels of the glass standing up, mirrored in X against the panel (`transform: mirror_x`), and is
  read through its interrupt line.
- Camera tiles, camera alerts and media artwork, kept in the PSRAM picture store (about 4 MB free). The classic ESP32
  has no JPEG engine, so the add-on sends these pictures as BMP, which the screen draws row by row as it arrives: a
  full-screen camera picture takes about 2.2 seconds, most of it the add-on preparing the picture. The download task's
  stack is in internal RAM, since the ESP32 allows no task stack in PSRAM (`psram_task.h`): internal RAM stays above
  125 KB free while pictures load.
- No full-screen snapshots (`ui_snapshot`): they need a second frame of the whole screen in internal RAM.
- LVGL draws through an eighth of the screen (`LVGL_BUFFER_SIZE: "8%"`, 38 KB), kept in internal RAM where the SPI bus
  reads it fastest. A full redraw takes 100-160 ms over the 40 MHz bus.

## Who tried what

[@notherealmarco](https://github.com/notherealmarco) ran this board file with ESPHome 2026.9.0, flashed over USB on
a WT32-SC01 (ESP32-D0WD revision 1.0, 16 MB flash): it boots, the touch chip answers (FT6336U, chip id 100), the
colours and the taps are right lying down, and the screen joins Wi-Fi and Home Assistant and shows its layout. A camera
tile opened full screen and a media player's cover show their pictures, with about 4 MB of PSRAM free and internal RAM
at 126 KB at its lowest. Not tried yet: standing up, camera alerts, and a full layout over a long run.

## Hardware references

| Part | Pins |
| --- | --- |
| Display (ST7796S, SPI) | clock GPIO14, MOSI GPIO13, MISO GPIO12, CS GPIO15, data/command GPIO21, reset GPIO22 |
| Touch (FT6336U, I2C 0x38) | SDA GPIO18, SCL GPIO19, interrupt GPIO39 |
| Backlight | GPIO23, PWM |

GPIO39 is input only and has no internal pull-up; ESPHome's ft63x6 asks for one anyway, so ESP-IDF logs
`gpio_pullup_en(85): GPIO number error (input-only pad has no internal PU)` once at boot. It is harmless: the board pulls
the line up itself.

- [ESPHome mipi_spi](https://esphome.io/components/display/mipi_spi/), model `ST7796`.
- [ESPHome ft63x6](https://esphome.io/components/touchscreen/ft63x6/).

## What to report while testing

1. A successful boot and appearance in Tessera.
2. Correct colours lying down and standing up.
3. Taps near each corner, slider drags and edge swipes, both ways up.
4. `sensor.<screen>_heap_free` and `sensor.<screen>_psram_free` with a full layout, any reset or Wi-Fi drop, and how
   long the screen stayed running.
