# Guition JC8012P4A1, 10.1 inch, its V2 and its V3

The **Guition JC8012P4A1** is a 10.1-inch 1280 x 800 IPS panel with a JD9365 MIPI-DSI driver, GSL3680 capacitive touch,
an ESP32-P4 with 16 MB flash and PSRAM, and an ESP32-C6 beside it for Wi-Fi. The first build of the board was added in
app 0.2.95. The **V2** and the **V3** are experimental boards of their own: their firmware is built and
rendered, but nobody on the project has run them on the glass yet.

## Which of the three

Guition ships this board in three builds that need different firmware:

- **The first build**: choose **Guition · 10.1 inch, JC8012P4A1**. Its ESP32-P4 is an early chip revision
  ("chip revision: v1.3" in the first lines of the USB log).
- **V2** (the label on the back says V2, and the number on the case is 2628 or higher): choose **Guition · 10.1 inch,
  JC8012P4A1 V2**. It has the first build's early ESP32-P4 ("chip revision: v1.3", `esp32p4-eco2` in the ROM line) and
  the newer LCD of the V3. On the first build's firmware this LCD shows a washed-out picture with a coloured line across
  it; the V3's firmware does not start on this chip.
- **V3** (the label on the board or its box says V3): choose **Guition · 10.1 inch, JC8012P4A1 V3**. It has a rev3
  ESP32-P4 and a newer LCD behind the same JD9365 driver.

The first build and the V2 differ only in their LCD, so a V2 on the first build's firmware starts and pairs, and only
the picture shows it. The chip revision is what separates the V3 from the other two. The two chip revisions are not
binary compatible: ESP-IDF lays out the chip's memory differently for them. A V3 that got
the first build's firmware never gets past its bootloader. The USB log then repeats these lines over and over:

```
ESP-ROM:esp32p4-eco7-...
rst:0x10 (CHIP_LP_WDT_RESET),boot:0x20f (SPI_FAST_FLASH_BOOT)
...
entry 0x4ff29eda
```

`eco7` in the ROM line means rev3 silicon. Flash the V3 firmware over USB and the screen starts. The other way round a
first-build board does not start on the V3 firmware either, and the first build's firmware brings it back.

### The V3 and ESPHome's display driver

On a V3, ESPHome 2026.9.0's own display driver (`mipi_dsi`) ends in an `abort()` while the display starts. It sets a
fixed clock for the display bus, and the rev3 chip does not accept it. The USB log then shows the firmware start, stop
straight after `i2c: Performing bus recovery` with `abort() was called at PC ... on core 1`, and restart until the
screen falls into safe mode. The V3 firmware before 0.6.1 did this.

ESPHome fixed it in its driver (esphome/esphome#18984, ESPHome 2026.9.1): ESP-IDF picks the clock that suits the chip.
Until the app builds with that version, the V3 board uses a copy of ESPHome 2026.9.0's driver with that one change,
`components/mipi_dsi_v3` (`platform: mipi_dsi_v3`). The first build and the other ESP32-P4 boards keep ESPHome's own
driver. When ESPHome's fix is in the app, the V3 goes back to `platform: mipi_dsi` and the copy goes away.

### The V3 and ESPHome's touch driver

On a V3, ESPHome 2026.9.0's own touch driver (`gsl3670`) loads the touch chip's firmware without an error and then
never reports a touch: the screen starts and shows its tiles, but nothing reacts to a finger. A pull-up on the interrupt
pin does not change that. The V3 firmware before 0.6.2 did this.

From firmware 0.6.2 the V3 runs the GSL3680 driver that the community configurations for this board use,
`components/gsl3680_v3` (`platform: gsl3680_v3`). It starts the chip with longer resets and checks that it runs, and
it loads the same firmware table. The V2 runs it too. The first build keeps ESPHome's `gsl3670`. `components/gsl3680_v3/README.md` says
where the driver comes from and what changed.

**Touch still does nothing after the update?** Take the screen off power completely for a few seconds: unplug it, and
take out the battery too if your board has one. The touch chip can get stuck in a state that a restart or a reset does
not clear, for example after another firmware ran on the board. The USB log then shows `Read RAM: 0, 0, 0, 0` where it
should show `5a, 5a, 5a, 5a`, and `touchscreen was marked as failed`. After a full power cycle the chip starts again.

### The Wi-Fi chip after a restart

The Wi-Fi is an ESP32-C6 beside the main chip, on an SDIO link, with firmware of its own (ESP-Hosted) that flashing
the screen does not change. On an ESP32-P4 a software restart (an update over the air, a restart from Home Assistant, a
crash) resets only the processors, not the SDIO link, and now and then the link came out of it stuck: the screen kept
restarting every 13 seconds and never came online, until its power went off. The USB log then shows `ESP-Hosted link
not yet up`, `sdmmc_host_wait_for_event returned 0x107` and `sdio_read_task: Failed to read data`, and later `OTA
rollback detected`: the bootloader went back to the previous firmware, which looped the same way. A cold start was
always fine. On a V3 here about one restart in five did this; other ESP32-P4 boards with a C6 (an M5Stack Tab5 in
another project, espressif/esp-hosted-mcu #240) show the same.

From the firmware after 0.57.0 (dev) every restart ends in a reset of the whole chip, as a cold start does
(`components/smart_display/wifi_chip.h`); a link that a crash leaves stuck costs one more restart, which then resets
the whole chip. The diagnostic sensor **Wi-Fi co-processor** in Home Assistant says what the C6 runs, for example
`ESP-Hosted 2.3.2 (host 2.12.12)`.

**The screen keeps restarting after an update and never comes online?** Take it off power for five seconds. It then
starts, possibly on the firmware it had before the update; update it again once it is online. With the firmware after
0.57.0 on the screen this no longer happens.

## Install

Update Tessera Screen Manager and choose the 10.1-inch Guition that matches your board in **New screen**. Follow
[Easy setup](EASY_SETUP.md) to create a profile with its own name, Wi-Fi references and unique API/OTA keys. Choose the
correct USB port, or download the firmware to flash with ESPHome Web from your own computer. Keep an existing working
profile if the board is already installed. Tessera adds it to Home Assistant by itself once it is on your Wi-Fi; then add its tiles.

A screen that is already in Tessera with the first build's profile but is really a V3 needs the V3 board chosen in a
new install; an update over the air cannot reach a screen that does not start. A V2 on the first build's profile does
start: install it again with the V2 board chosen, over USB or from the browser.

The remote packages are `packages/jc8012p4a1.yaml`, `packages/jc8012p4a1v2.yaml` and `packages/jc8012p4a1v3.yaml`; the
checkout entries are the files of the same names under `checkout/`. Each combines `packages/core.yaml` with its board
file, `packages/boards/guition-jc8012p4a1.yaml`, `guition-jc8012p4a1-v2.yaml` or `guition-jc8012p4a1-v3.yaml`. What they share with the
7-inch Guition (the ESP32-P4 and its ESP32-C6) lives in `packages/hardware/guition-esp32p4.yaml`, and the V2 and the V3 take
their touch panel from `packages/hardware/guition-jc8012p4a1.yaml`. The first build keeps its own touch panel in its
board file, because it uses a different touch driver.

## Layout and capabilities

- 1280 x 800 at 149 dpi: five columns of five, lying down and standing up (firmware 0.18.0; five by four and four by
  five before). A layout saved on the old grid moves on to the new one by itself when the screen reports it, every
  tile on its own page, row and column. The panel itself is
  800 x 1280, so LVGL turns the picture and the touch a quarter.
- Five to eight rows lying down (app 0.4.59, firmware 0.35.0, GitHub #125, thanks to
  [Jeroen Peters](https://github.com/Heronimonimo) in #151), and since firmware 0.53.0 any grid up to eight by eight
  from the Grid button beside the pages in the editor, without a new build. Each row more puts five more tiles on a
  page and makes the tiles lower: a tile is 23.5 mm high with five rows and 14.0 mm with eight. The layout moves along
  by itself. Standing up starts on five by five.

<img src="images/jc8012p4a1-7rows.png" width="100%" alt="A living room on the 10.1-inch Guition with seven rows, thirty-five cells, rendered from the firmware">

- The backlight is a PWM pin (GPIO23): brightness is a percentage, and standby, night mode, Sleep, Wake and the alert's
  flashes are enabled.
- Camera tiles, full-screen snapshots, live tile pictures, camera alerts and media artwork use the shared PSRAM
  implementation.
- The GSL3680 reports pixel coordinates, with no calibration. The existing touch filter and action guard stay in use.
- The speaker and the microphone are the screen's audio on all three builds, experimental: a media player in Home
  Assistant for announcements and music, Volume and Microphone under Screen settings, and what audio plugins (a voice
  assistant) need. See "Speaker and microphone" below.

## Speaker and microphone

The board has one ES8311 codec, an NS4150 amplifier with a two-pin speaker connector, and one analogue MEMS
microphone. There is no ES7210 and no second microphone. The three board files take the audio every Guition ESP32-P4
panel shares (`packages/hardware/guition-p4-audio.yaml`), with the pins of the 10.1-inch circuit board:

- The speaker and the microphone work at the same time, at 16 kHz, the rate Guition's own firmware uses. The codec has
  one I2S bus, and two of the chip's I2S ports share its clock pins: the microphone's port gives the clocks and the
  speaker's follows them (ESPHome's `i2s_mode: secondary`). The microphone's port runs from the start, so the speaker
  always has its clock, also when nothing listens. There is no echo cancellation: the board has nothing that hears
  what the speaker plays, so a voice assistant hears the speaker too.
- The bus runs all the time, in silence between sounds, so the amplifier stays on. Switching it with every sound cut
  off the start of a short one, such as the click of a tap.
- The volume stops at the codec's 0 dB. Above it a loud sound clips, and a community member found full volume
  distorting and browning out a V2.
- The microphone is analogue, on the ES8311's own input, so the codec keeps its analogue input (`use_microphone: false`
  in ESPHome's `es8311`, which would switch it to a PDM microphone) and the microphone is read on the left I2S slot
  with the codec's digital gain at 24 dB, Guition's own setting.

This is **experimental**. [@Eugen417](https://github.com/Eugen417) runs the same wiring, both ways at once with the
microphone's port giving the clocks, on a V3 with a build of his own (#259): the tap sound and a voice assistant
plugin work. The first build and the V2 have the same circuit board and pins, and have not run it yet.

## The camera

The camera version of this board (the "C" in Guition's model name, JC8012P4A1C) carries an OmniVision OV02C10 on the
MIPI-CSI port. It answers on the touch bus at 0x36, clocks itself and has no reset or power-down pin; the board file
names that bus in `CAMERA_I2C`, the feature `camera_sensor` (docs/PLUGINS.md). Espressif's own `esp_cam_sensor` has no
driver for the OV02C10, so the Screen camera plugin brings one (its `components/ov02c10`, with an image tuning for this
sensor) beside the camera driver, and makes it a camera of the screen's device in Home Assistant: a 1288 x 728 picture,
a stream at up to five pictures a second.

- **A board without the camera** fits Screen camera as well: `CAMERA_I2C` says where a camera answers, not that one is
  there. Without a sensor the camera only logs that it found none.
- **Tried on the glass:** the V3 shows the camera in Home Assistant with auto exposure, white balance, mirror and
  flip (Heronimonimo, ivanfmartinez). The first build and the V2 carry the same camera on the same pins; the first
  build ran it in sullb/esphome-p4-csi-camera. Neither has run Screen camera yet.
- **A protective film** may come on the lens of a new board and make the picture hazy.

## Hardware references

- [ESPHome MIPI DSI](https://esphome.io/components/display/mipi_dsi/): the first build uses ESPHome's
  `model: JC8012P4A1`, the V2 and the V3 `model: JC8012P4A1-V2` (ESPHome 2026.8.0 and later), the newer LCD's init table
  that the community configurations for both builds run. The V2 builds it through ESPHome's own `mipi_dsi`, the V3
  through `platform: mipi_dsi_v3` for now (see "The V3 and ESPHome's display driver" above).
- Touch: reset GPIO22, interrupt GPIO21, on I2C SDA GPIO7 and SCL GPIO8. The first build uses ESPHome's `gsl3670`
  platform with `model: GUITION-JC8012P4A1`, the V2 and the V3 `platform: gsl3680_v3` (see "The V3 and ESPHome's touch driver"
  above).
- Wi-Fi: ESP32-C6 over four-bit SDIO, reset GPIO54, CMD GPIO19, CLK GPIO18, D0 to D3 GPIO14 to GPIO17.
- Audio: ES8311 at 0x18 on the touch panel's I2C bus. I2S MCLK GPIO13, BCLK GPIO12, LRCLK GPIO10, speaker data GPIO9
  (the codec's DSDIN), microphone data GPIO11 (its ASDOUT). Amplifier enable GPIO20, high for on, with a pull-down that
  keeps it off at reset. This follows Guition's own documents in its
  [P4-series repository](https://github.com/guitionofficial/P4-series/tree/9f1510954d281e30b19cd8baf115e2ed2a65a90a):
  the JC8012P4A1C_I_W_Y schematic (`5-Schematic/7_CODEC.png` and `3_ESP32-P4.png`) and the pins in its board support
  package (`esp32_p4_function_ev_board.h`). The community firmware [HomeTiles](https://github.com/GalusPeres/HomeTiles)
  drives the speaker of these boards the same way (`src/devices/*/speaker_board.h`).
- Chip: `engineering_sample: true` for the first build and the V2, `false` for the V3. All three run the CPU at 360 MHz.
- ESPHome 2026.9.0 or newer, for the audio.

## What to report while testing a V2 or a V3

1. The first lines of the USB log (the ROM line and the chip revision), a successful boot and appearance in
   Tessera.
2. Correct colours and a stable picture across several page changes, a restart from the screen's settings, an update
   over the air and a cold start.
3. Touch: tap the four corners and drag across the glass. A tap that lands mirrored or turned says the touch transform
   of this build differs from the first one.
4. `sensor.<screen>_heap_free` after a first boot with a full layout.

On any of the three builds, for the speaker and the microphone:

1. A text-to-speech announcement and music on the screen's media player, without a voice assistant plugin as well, the
   Volume setting from low to high, and whether the speaker hisses between sounds. Report any restart at a high volume.
2. The microphone, with a voice assistant plugin: whether the wake word is heard from across the room, also while
   music plays.
